01 the claim

One student record,
from admission
to graduation.

Termwise runs a college’s daily records: admissions, attendance, marks, fees, staff leave, and the reports the university, AICTE and NAAC ask for. It is built for classrooms with weak Wi-Fi and an office that works in tables all day.

open the demo read the guide password termwise-demo

The demo runs on a free instance, so the first load can take a minute. Every campus, student and receipt here is synthetic.

02 in class

A class is marked in five taps, with the Wi-Fi off.

Everyone starts present. One tap marks absent, a second late, a third present again. A class of sixty with five absentees takes five taps, a save and a confirm.

Ankit Panda is 6.7 points under the cutoff. The daily job flags him once, as a mentor task; the flag changes no record and sends nothing to the student.

Attendance is stored as one session per batch, subject, date and period, with a UUID the phone creates while it is offline. A lab is one session covering several periods, and percentages are weighted by periods.

Late counts as attended. The cutoff is a setting on the programme, not a constant in the code.

03 sync

The sheet saves on the phone and settles itself later.

Attendance in a room with no signal, without overwriting the sheet another teacher already saved.

  1. queue

    Save into the outbox

    Today’s classes and rosters, names and roll numbers only, sit in IndexedDB. A saved sheet waits in an outbox and the class shows as queued.

  2. send

    Flush when there is a network

    The outbox sends when the app opens, when the network returns, or through Background Sync on Android. Each item is decided on its own, so one bad sheet never blocks the rest.

  3. decide

    Four answers, never a guess

    saved
    written against the session
    stale
    an older edit, ignored
    conflict
    another teacher saved first, so the sheet goes to the office queue
    rejected
    marked by a different teacher on a shared phone

    Edit times are corrected for a wrong phone clock, using the gap between the phone and the server at send.

04 the office

Four offices, one row per student.

Admissions, exams, accounts and HR each see their own modules, and every change is written to the audit log in the same transaction.

marks
Draft, submitted, locked. A reopen needs a typed reason, and a locked subject rejects edits at the server. An accepted appeal can correct one locked mark.
fees
Due is instalment minus scholarship minus payments, floored at zero. Nothing is stored that can drift from the receipts, and a payment over the remaining due is refused.
receipts
Each payment prints with the amount in words, a keyed HMAC over its fixed fields and a QR code. The public page recomputes the hash and shows the student’s name masked.
reports
University upload CSV, Tally receipt vouchers, and the AICTE and NAAC returns, all built from the same rows the screens read.

Students and parents sign in to the same record. Every portal query is pinned to the signed-in student or a linked child, so no student id is ever taken from the browser.

A parent sees each child’s attendance, marks and dues, approves leave before the mentor, and gives or withdraws consent for a minor.

05 the rules

Rules decide who is at risk. The model words, plans and refuses.

The assistant is the part most likely to be wrong, so it is built to show its work and to be unable to write.

the flag
Attendance under the cutoff in any subject, a drop of ten points over four weeks, or internal marks under the configured share. The rule decides; a flag opens a mentor task and changes nothing else. With too little data it reports not enough data, never clear.
the question
A plain-English question becomes a plan: SQL, the filters in plain English and a one-line summary. The SQL is parsed with the Postgres parser and checked against an allowlist of six views and about twenty functions. No writes, no comments, one statement.
the boundary
It runs as a separate read-only role, inside a read-only transaction, on views that filter themselves by campus, role, staff, student and department. Every answer shows the filters, the row count and the SQL.

06 run it

Clone, seed, sign in.

The demo runs on Postgres, so the database starts in Docker. Seed data and demo logins are written by the seed script. 

git clone https://github.com/moneytosms/termwise &amp;&amp; cd termwise
pnpm install
docker compose up -d db
pnpm db:migrate &amp;&amp; pnpm db:seed
pnpm dev

Node 24 or later, pnpm 12.15 and Postgres 18. Open http://localhost:3000 and sign in as eng.admin@termwise.demo or cse.faculty@termwise.demo; every demo password is termwise-demo. The guide carries the full deployment steps, the env vars and the persona list.

07 limits

What this is not.

  • Synthetic data. Every account and record is made up for the demo. The three seed students show the marks, attendance and fee rules at work.
  • Messages are logged, not sent. No SMS or WhatsApp gateway is in scope, and the Odia parent templates need a native speaker’s review.
  • Payments are test mode. No gateway, no real money.
  • The assistant needs a key. It runs on Gemini or Groq with a daily call cap. Without a key it says so and points to the normal lists; risk reasons keep the rule’s own wording.
  • Verification shows a minimum. The public receipt and mark sheet pages show the totals and a masked name, never the record.
